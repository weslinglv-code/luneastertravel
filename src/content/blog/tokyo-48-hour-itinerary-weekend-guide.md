## Day 1: West Tokyo — Shinjuku, Shibuya & Harajuku

### Morning: Shinjuku Gyoen & Government Building

Start at **Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden** (¥500 entry, opens 9 AM). It's one of Tokyo's largest parks and the perfect jet-lag recovery spot. Spend an hour walking the English Landscape and French Formal gardens.

Then walk 10 minutes to the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** — the observation deck on the 45th floor is **free** and offers sweeping views of the city (and Mt. Fuji on clear days).

### Lunch: Omoide Yokocho

Head to **Omoide Yokocho** (Memory Lane), a narrow alley of tiny yakitori stalls near Shinjuku Station. Grab skewers of chicken, pork, and vegetables for under ¥1,500 ($10). It's atmospheric, authentic, and quick.

### Afternoon: Harajuku & Shimokitazawa

Take the JR Yamanote Line two stops to **Harajuku**. Walk down **Takeshita Street** for crepes and quirky fashion, then escape the crowds into **Meiji Shrine** — a serene forested sanctuary in the middle of the city (free entry).

If you have time, hop one more stop to **Shimokitazawa** — Tokyo's vintage clothing and record district. Great for thrift shopping and indie cafes.

### Evening: Shibuya Crossing & Dinner

End the day at **Shibuya Crossing**. Snap the classic photo from the Starbucks window (arrive before 7 PM to get a seat), then explore the surrounding alleys for dinner.

**Food pick:** **Uobei Sushi** — high-quality conveyor belt sushi with a fun touchscreen ordering system. Plates start at ¥110 ($0.75). Expect to spend ¥1,500–¥2,500 ($10–$17).

> **Stay nearby:** Book a hotel in Shinjuku through [Booking.com](#aff:booking_tokyo_shinjuku) — it's the best transport hub for a weekend trip, with direct airport access and hundreds of restaurants within walking distance.

## Day 2: East Tokyo — Asakusa, Akihabara & Ueno

### Morning: Senso-ji Temple

Start early at **Senso-ji** (opens 6 AM, free entry) — Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645 AD. Walk through the **Kaminarimon Gate** with its giant red lantern, then browse **Nakamise Shopping Street** for traditional snacks: ningyo-yaki (doll-shaped cakes), senbei (rice crackers), and matcha ice cream.

### Late Morning: teamLab Planets

Book **teamLab Planets** for 11 AM — an immersive digital art museum where you walk barefoot through water and light installations. This is one of Tokyo's most popular attractions, so **book at least 3 days in advance**.

> **Skip the line:** Get your [teamLab Planets tickets via Klook](#aff:klook_tokyo_disney) — often cheaper than walk-up and you bypass the queue.

### Lunch: Asakusa Street Food

Grab lunch in Asakusa before heading to Akihabara. Try **tempura donburi** at a local shop — a bowl of shrimp and vegetable tempura over rice for around ¥1,000 ($7).

### Afternoon: Akihabara

Take the Tsukuba Express from Asakusa to **Akihabara** (5 minutes, ¥170). Even if you're not into anime or gaming, the energy is infectious. Browse multi-floor arcades, check out Yodobashi Camera (a massive electronics store), and grab a crepe at a maid cafe if you're feeling adventurous.

### Evening: Ueno Park & Final Dinner

End your 48 hours at **Ueno Park** — especially beautiful during cherry blossom season (late March–early April) or autumn foliage (late November–early December). The park is free and open 24 hours.

**Final dinner:** **Ameyoko Street** under the Ueno train tracks — a bustling market street with cheap eats, from fresh seafood to Korean BBQ. Budget ¥2,000–¥3,000 ($13–$20).

## Getting Around Tokyo in 48 Hours

### Metro Pass

Tokyo Metro offers a **24-hour pass** for ¥800 ($5.50) — unlimited rides on all 13 Metro lines. If you're doing both East and West Tokyo in one day, this pays for itself after 3 rides.

> **Pro tip:** For the JR Yamanote Line (which connects Shinjuku, Shibuya, Harajuku, Ueno, and Akihabara), you'll need a separate **Suica** or **ICOCA** IC card. Load ¥2,000 ($13) for the weekend — any leftover balance can be refunded.

### Pocket WiFi

Don't rely on your home carrier's roaming. Get a [Pocket WiFi](#aff:klook_pocket_wifi) from Klook — unlimited 4G for your entire trip, pick up at Narita or Haneda airport.
