## Day 1-3: Tokyo — The Sensory Overload

### Where to Stay in Tokyo

For first-timers, **Shinjuku** is the best base. It's the transport hub of Tokyo, with direct trains from Narita and Haneda airports. You'll find hundreds of restaurants within walking distance, and the nightlife is unmatched.

> **Tip:** Book your hotel through [Booking.com](#aff:booking_tokyo_shinjuku) — they often have free cancellation options, which is great if your plans change.

### Day 1: Shinjuku & Shibuya

Start at the **Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden** — it's free, peaceful, and the perfect jet-lag recovery spot. Then walk to **Shibuya Crossing** (the world's busiest intersection) and snap the classic photo from the Starbucks window.

In the evening, explore **Omoide Yokocho** (Memory Lane) — a narrow alley of tiny yakitori stalls where you can eat and drink like a local for under $20.

### Day 2: Asakusa & teamLab Planets

**Asakusa** is Tokyo's old town. Visit **Senso-ji Temple** (Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645 AD), then browse **Nakamise Shopping Street** for traditional snacks and souvenirs.

In the afternoon, book **teamLab Planets** — an immersive digital art museum where you walk barefoot through water and light installations. This is one of Tokyo's most popular attractions, so **book in advance** to skip the line.

### Day 3: Harajuku & Shimokitazawa

Skip the crowds at Takeshita Street and head straight to **Cat Street** — the quieter backstreet where Tokyo's street fashion was born. Then spend the afternoon in **Shimokitazawa**, a bohemian neighborhood full of vintage shops, record stores, and cozy cafes.

## Transport: The JR Pass Question

The biggest question first-timers ask: **Is the Japan Rail Pass worth it?**

For this 7-day itinerary, the answer is **yes** — but only if you buy the right version.

The [7-Day JR Pass](#aff:klook_jrpass) costs about $270 (as of 2026). A one-way Tokyo → Kyoto Shinkansen ticket is ~$80, and you'll use the pass for local JR trains in Tokyo and Osaka too. If you're doing Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka, the pass pays for itself.

> **Important:** You must buy the JR Pass **before arriving in Japan**. Order it online through [Klook](#aff:klook_jrpass) and pick up the exchange order at the airport.

### Pocket WiFi: Don't Skip This

Google Maps is essential in Japan — train stations are massive and signage can be confusing. Rent a [Pocket WiFi device](#aff:klook_pocket_wifi) and pick it up at the airport. It's cheaper than roaming and keeps your whole group connected.

> **Don't forget:** A [compact power bank](#aff:amazon_powerbank) is essential — you'll be using Google Maps, translating menus, and taking photos all day. The Anker PowerCore 10000 is small enough to fit in any pocket and gives you 3 full phone charges. Pair it with a [universal travel adapter](#aff:amazon_adapter) and you're fully set.

## Day 4: Mount Fuji & Hakone

Take a day trip from Tokyo to see **Mount Fuji**. The easiest option is a [guided day tour](#aff:klook_mt_fuji) — it handles all the logistics (bus, lunch, Lake Ashi cruise) for about $100–$130.

If you prefer DIY: take the JR Chuo Line to **Otsuki**, then the Fuji Excursion train to **Kawaguchiko Station**. The view of Fuji from Lake Kawaguchi is iconic.

**Pro tip:** Fuji is only visible clearly about 60% of days. Check the [Mt. Fuji Visibility Forecast](https://fuji-san.info/) before you go. If it's cloudy, swap this day with Day 3.

## Day 5-6: Kyoto — The Spiritual Heart

Take the morning Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto (2 hours 15 minutes with your JR Pass).

### Where to Stay in Kyoto

Stay near **Kyoto Station** for easy transport access, or in **Gion** if you want the traditional atmosphere. [Browse Kyoto hotels here](#aff:booking_kyoto).

### Day 5: Fushimi Inari & Arashiyama

Start early — like **7 AM early** — at **Fushimi Inari Taisha**. The famous thousand torii gates are magical at sunrise, and you'll have them almost to yourself. By 10 AM, it's packed with tourists.

In the afternoon, head to **Arashiyama** on the western edge of Kyoto. The **bamboo grove** is the highlight, but don't miss **Tenryu-ji Temple** next door — its garden is one of the most beautiful in Japan.

### Day 6: Higashiyama & Gion

Spend the day walking the **Higashiyama district** — a preserved old neighborhood with sloping streets, traditional shops, and temples. The route: **Kiyomizu-dera → Sannenzaka → Yasaka Shrine → Gion**.

In Gion at dusk, you might spot a **geisha** (called *geiko* in Kyoto) rushing to an evening engagement. Be respectful — no flash photography.

> **Local secret:** Most tourists rush through Kyoto in one day. If you have time, our [Kyoto Takao Three Temples guide](/products/kyoto-takao-three-temples) reveals a hidden maple-leaf valley just 30 minutes from central Kyoto — almost no tourists, even in peak season.

## Day 7: Osaka — Eat Your Way Out

Take the JR Special Rapid from Kyoto to Osaka (30 minutes). Drop your bags and head straight to **Dotonbori** — the neon-lit food street that's Osaka's soul.

### Must-Eat in Osaka

- **Takoyaki** — octopus balls, crispy outside, molten inside
- **Okonomiyaki** — savory cabbage pancake, Osaka-style
- **Kushikatsu** — deep-fried skewers, dip once in the sauce (no double-dipping!)

In the afternoon, visit **Osaka Castle** — the grounds are free, and the castle museum has great views from the top floor.

### Where to Stay in Osaka

**Namba** or **Umeda** are the best areas. [Find Osaka hotels here](#aff:booking_osaka).
